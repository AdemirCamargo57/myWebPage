<div class="page-heading"><span class="eyebrow">Academic profile</span><h1>About me</h1><p class="deck">A career in physical chemistry, mathematics, and the computational study of molecules.</p></div>

<article class="prose page-body" markdown="1">

I am Ademir João Camargo, a professor and researcher at the State University of Goiás (UEG), Brazil. My work focuses on theoretical and computational chemistry, bringing together quantum mechanics, electronic structure methods, and molecular dynamics to investigate chemical systems.

I earned my degree in Chemistry at the Federal University of Goiás in 1987 and a degree in Pure Mathematics at the Federal University of São Carlos in 2000. At the University of São Paulo, I completed a master's degree in Chemistry in 1998 and a doctorate in Chemistry in 2001, both under the supervision of Milan Trsic. My graduate research examined molecular structure, biological activity, and the quantum chemical description of organic and inorganic compounds with potential technological applications.

From 2002 to 2003, I carried out postdoctoral research at the Federal University of São Carlos, supported by the São Paulo Research Foundation (FAPESP). This work included the computational study of materials with nonlinear optical properties.

I joined UEG in 2003, initially teaching in Iporá. In 2004, I moved to the university's Anápolis campus after a public faculty selection process. My teaching has included general and physical chemistry, quantum chemistry, experimental physical chemistry, calculus, differential equations, real analysis, and complex variables. I also teach graduate topics in electronic structure and molecular dynamics.

My research examines how molecular structure and interactions determine physicochemical behavior. I use density functional theory, ab initio molecular dynamics, and statistical analysis to investigate solvation, chemical reactivity, bioactive compounds, and materials. My interests also include excited-state dynamics and photochemical processes.

Alongside research and teaching, I develop computational tools for molecular analysis and simulation. I share these programs through GitHub and make my teaching notes available here for students and researchers.
