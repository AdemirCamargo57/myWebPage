## Electronic structure and molecular properties

Quantum chemical calculations connect molecular structure with observable properties. My work uses electronic structure methods, including Kohn–Sham density functional theory, to investigate chemical bonding, charge distributions, frontier orbitals, electrostatic potentials, and the properties of organic and inorganic compounds.

These calculations support studies of bioactive molecules and materials with potential technological applications, including nonlinear optical behavior. Statistical methods such as principal component analysis, partial least squares, and hierarchical cluster analysis help relate molecular descriptors to activity and other properties.

## Molecular dynamics and solvation

The behavior of a molecule depends on its surroundings and changes over time. Ab initio molecular dynamics provides a way to study these effects while describing the electronic structure quantum mechanically.

My research uses Car–Parrinello and Born–Oppenheimer molecular dynamics to examine solute–solvent interactions, hydrogen bonding, ion complexation, and the influence of the environment on molecular geometry and electronic properties.

## Reactivity and excited-state dynamics

Theoretical characterization of transition structures helps describe reaction pathways and kinetics. For photochemical processes, surface-hopping approaches allow us to investigate coupled electronic and nuclear motion, the evolution of excited states, and possible reaction outcomes following excitation.
